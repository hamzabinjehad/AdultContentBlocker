About the quotes

Old books are quoted exactly, from public-domain translations (Long's Marcus Aurelius, Gummere's Seneca, Müller's Dhammapada, Legge's Lao Tzu, Carter's and Higginson's Epictetus, Ross's Aristotle, Trotter's Pascal, Mozley's Ovid, Giles's Sun Tzu, Jowett's Plato), plus Thoreau, Emerson, Franklin, William James, Samuel Smiles, Frederick Douglass, Thomas Paine and Theodore Roosevelt. Each was checked against the source text. Popular lines whose wording was later changed are left out: “Most powerful is he who has himself in his own power”, “Very little is needed to make a happy life”, “The chains of habit are too weak to be felt…”, Muhammad Ali's “I hated every minute of training…” (no confirmed source) and the popular form of Mike Tyson's “Everybody has a plan…” (his 1987 words were different). Modern books (Dopamine Nation, Easyway, EasyPeasy, Irresistible, Digital Minimalism, Indistractable, Stolen Focus) are under copyright, so their ideas are in our own words, set in plain type, with “Idea from …”.
