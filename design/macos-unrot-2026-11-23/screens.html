The blocking system

How a block is decided, and where it lands

One engine on the Mac makes every decision. Apps and browsers only ask it and show the answer, so there is one set of rules and no second place to switch them off.

An app comes to the front

  1. The Mac app notices which app is in front and counts its time.
  2. The engine decides: allow, pause first, or closed, and why.
  3. A panel covers that app's window, not the whole screen.
  4. What you choose is recorded: an open, extra minutes, or walking away.

A page opens in a browser

  1. The extension checks the host against the short list the app gave it. Other pages are never asked about.
  2. It asks the same engine through the bridge. Only the address goes across; no page text, no title.
  3. The answer lands in the tab: left alone, feed hidden, or the pause or closed page.
  4. Every 30 seconds it looks again, so a limit that runs out or quiet hours that begin close an open tab too.

Adult content, underneath all of it

  1. The Shield does not depend on any rule. Lists, words and image checks run on their own.
  2. It fails closed. If the app goes silent during a lock, the browser tightens.
  3. Distraction rules fail open. A rule that cannot be read never closes a page by accident.
  4. Never-closed sites skip rules and Focus, but never the Shield.
