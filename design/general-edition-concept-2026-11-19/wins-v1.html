Your wins: before Unrot, and now

No flame, no score, nothing that resets. Progress is shown three honest ways: you before Unrot vs you now, measured by Android; time given back, a wall of hour tiles that only fills up; and personal bests taken from your own days. Research: tracking progress helps people reach goals, more so when it's recorded and visible (Harkin et al. 2016, 138 studies). Effort rises near a goal (goal gradient, Kivetz 2006). Resetting streaks cause "streak anxiety", and people drop the app.

Home-screen widgets. "Given back", which only grows. "Today so far" against where the old you would be by this hour, which works as a nudge mid-day. "Before → now" in a wide size. Tapping any of them opens Your wins.

Where "before" comes from

Android keeps several weeks of app use. On the day Unrot is set up, it saves one number per measure from the weeks before (feed time, opens, first and last scroll). Nothing else is stored. Without enough history, your first week becomes "before", and the page says so.

Time given back, honestly

Each day adds (before − that day) when it's lower. A heavier day adds nothing and takes nothing away, so the wall never empties, and "before" stays your own number, never an average of other people.

Feel

Tiles pop in one by one when you open Today; the newest hour is red. Reaching a round number (a day, a week back) plays the celebrate chime and three-beat vibration, once. Everything holds still with "Remove animations".
