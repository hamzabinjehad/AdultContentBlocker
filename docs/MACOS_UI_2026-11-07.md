# Mac UI pass — 7 November 2026

A second pass over the Mac window after the 6 November refresh. The previous
pass borrowed ScreenZen's *layout* — that part stays — but also its warm-paper,
indigo and navy palette. This pass keeps the principle and drops the palette:
the window now uses the Mac's own neutral surfaces and one system blue accent.
It also removes the explanatory paragraphs from the page. Anything that needs
to explain itself lives behind an **Explain** button.

This is a surface pass. No protection rule, lock rule, consent sentence,
allowance rule, partner path or commitment deadline changed.

## What was wrong

- The warm paper, indigo accent and navy hero were a borrowed colour scheme,
  not Hisn's. They made the Mac app look like a web dashboard rather than a Mac
  app next to Finder, Mail or System Settings.
- The pages still carried long explanations under titles, cards and controls:
  a paragraph under the network heading, two under browser protection, three
  under visual checks, a sentence under almost every toggle.
- Every explanation competed with the control it described.

## The change

### One neutral surface, one system accent

`HisnTheme.swift` now holds explicit light/dark values for the canvas, the
sidebar, a card, a raised well and the hairlines. There is no warm paper, no
indigo and no navy. The accent is macOS system blue; green, orange and red are
the system status colours. State is still carried by a word and a symbol first.

Type stays in the system face: no display face, no rounded headings, no
monospaced countdown styling beyond digits. The Mac's own text is the design.

### Explain instead of paragraphs

`HnExplain` is a small blue button that opens a popover. It is used for:

- the purpose of every page, beside the title;
- the meaning of every page section (`PageSection` turns its subtitle into an
  Explain button);
- each mode, toggle, list and setup step that has a reason;
- the network, browser-guard and visual-check caveats.

The visible page now keeps only the state, the control, the action and a short
warning when something is wrong. The long sentence still exists — it is one
click away, in the same words it had before, so the Arabic catalog did not lose
meaning.

### The window

- The sidebar uses the app's own row: system-blue selection, hover, and a
  compact protection footer. It reads as a Mac sidebar without the opaque
  native selection bar that hid the row label in the render host.
- The page header is the page name, with Explain beside it. The old subtitle
  under the header is gone.
- Cards are flat control-background surfaces with a 10-point radius and a
  separator hairline. No gradients, no shadows, no deep hero.
- The Overview status card is a normal card: a state symbol, the state word, the
  ready ratio, the four layer rows, and one filled action. The ratio and the
  layer words carry the state; colour is a third signal.
- The Lock page keeps its choices and its consent, but mode descriptions are
  behind Explain; the pinned action bar says "Enforces 2 of 4 layers" or
  "Blocks nothing until setup is finished."
- The browser-guard countdown panel uses the same dark neutral surface and
  system blue, not the old navy/indigo.
- The pause keeps its one calm surface, now following the system appearance.

## Verification

- `./test.sh macos` — **580 tests, 0 failures**; the XLIFF check reports
  **741 strings, all with Arabic**.
- Captures reviewed in light and dark, at 1120 and 760 points, and in Arabic
  RTL.

## Not claimed

- No user study, task-time measurement or VoiceOver audit.
- No change to blocking policy, the commitment model, partner release or
  consent copy.
- The render host does not carry the app bundle's chosen language, so runtime
  `String(localized:)` values stay English in the Arabic capture. The catalog
  and the `ar.lproj` test cover the Arabic strings themselves.
