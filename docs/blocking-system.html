One pipeline.
Two sizes of list.

A domain becomes a block only after it survives cleaning, subdomain collapse, a safety rail and a signature. The same build produces the browser’s core list and the Mac’s full list.

Most specific wins.
Ties go to the block.

The same order is asserted on the Mac and in the browser by one shared fixture. A disagreement between them is silent, so the contract is tested, not assumed.

    Built-in blocks

    Yandex search hosts and anonymous X/Twitter viewers. Bundled policy, not a list entry — and not something an allowance can override.

Mode

Strict mode inverts the question.

Everything is denied unless it is on the allowlist. This is the only mode that approaches “unknown = blocked”.

  • BrowserAn allowed page may load its plumbing — scripts, styles, fonts, images, fetches — from any host that is not itself blocked. Frames, media and sockets still need their own allowance.
  • macOSNo plumbing carve-out. A hostname the filter cannot identify, or a bare IP, is denied.
  • PhonesNo strict allowlist mode. App shields, Screen Time, DNS and the commitment do the work.

One escape valve: the allowlist. No silent exceptions.

04

What each layer
actually buys you.

Features are not a checklist of independent toggles. They are coverage in different places — destination, content, time, app, network.

Signed domain lists

Two tiers from one build: 236,586 core and 5,280,200 full. Ed25519 manifest, per-artifact SHA-256, monotonic version, bundled seed fallback.

Browser · Mac · iOS · Android · Router

Adult TLD rail

.adult, .porn, .sex and .xxx are refused without a list entry. .cam stays open because it has ordinary camera uses.

Every surface

Hostname terms

116 reviewed terms catch arab-sex-tube or sharmota123. Token terms never match essex; never-keywords protect milford and pornic.

Browser · Mac · Android

URL rules

Keywords with explicit boundaries, site-specific paths, reviewed obfuscations and percent-encoded Arabic queries. They judge the URL before a request is made.

Browser · Mac (partly)

Page-text scoring

Zones are weighted, occurrences capped, and a page must clear both a threshold (7.5 at default) and a density floor (2.0 per 1,000 words). Language-aware, Arabic-first.

Browser · iOS Safari · Android apps

Per-post filtering

On X/Twitter, each rendered post is scored on its own. A bad post is hidden and its media paused — without blocking the whole platform.

Browser · iOS Safari

Visual checks

Opt-in and local. Media is hidden before inspection, classified with NSFWJS, then revealed or kept hidden. Unreadable media stays unknown — never “clean”.

Browser · iOS Safari · Android 14+

SafeSearch

Query transforms for Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo and Brave, plus YouTube’s restricted header. On Android DNS, exact search hosts resolve to a safe CNAME.

Browser · Android DNS · Network

Short-video blocking

Opt-in blocks for YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels/Explore, Facebook Reels and Snapchat Spotlight — direct URLs plus in-page route changes.

Browser · Android

Apps & time

Mac app blocking, iOS app/category shields with daily budgets, quiet hours and a pause rule, Android selected apps, limits, schedules and browser coverage.

Mac · iOS · Android

DNS & network

Android’s DNS tunnel, an optional DoH profile, router AdGuard rules and a hosts fallback. Coverage for devices that never run Hisn — on that network.

Android · iOS · Mac · Router

Locks & fail-closed

A deadline the filter cannot shorten, a 48-hour self-release, partner approval, and a browser that clamps to strict after five minutes of app silence during a lock.

05

A lock is a rule
about changing the rules.

The hard part is not blocking a site at 9pm. It is making the decision hard to reverse at 2am, without pretending the device owner has no recovery powers.

The commitment

Slow, social, and honest about its exits.

A deadline lives in a root-owned authority and is mirrored. Winding the clock back freezes the countdown instead of ending it. A self-release waits 48 hours; a partner can approve an earlier one.

Time lock

Deadline mirrored across stores, read as the maximum. Strict cannot become standard while locked.

Partner release

An Ed25519 approval from a key the filter also trusts. The partner learns a release was requested — nothing else.

Fail closed

No native app for five minutes during a lock → the browser behaves as strict with its last allowlist.

Private by default

No browsing log. The Mac records an aggregate daily count — never a host, app or address.

“The failure mode of a self-control tool is not ‘the user is annoyed’. It is ‘the tool quietly stopped working and nobody noticed.’” — extension/background.js
06

What it still
cannot see.

These are documented product boundaries, not bugs. A blocklist is a race; a classifier is a guess; a device owner has recovery powers.

01

A new generic domain

example.some-new-tld is on no list, and the TLD is not adult-sponsored. Strict allowlist is the only complete answer.

02

Content inside a mixed site

X, Reddit and Telegram are allowed destinations. Per-post scoring helps on X; blocking the app or strict mode helps everywhere else.

03

Images and video

Domain and text layers cannot see pixels. Visual checks are opt-in, sample media, and can miss protected or unreadable content.

04

Encrypted native traffic

The Mac filter sees a flow, not the post inside it. There is no TLS interception, by design.

05

Another browser or device

Each layer has a scope. The browser guard and network DNS narrow it; they do not turn one Mac into every device.

06

VPNs and DoH

Android routes known encrypted resolvers and blocks provider names, but an unlisted resolver, another VPN or a direct IP can still go around DNS.

07

An administrator

The person is the admin of their own machine. Guardian accounts, supervision and MDM raise the cost; recovery mode and erasure remain.

08

100% coverage

No combination of lists, DNS, text and visual checks guarantees all content. Hisn says so before you rely on it.
